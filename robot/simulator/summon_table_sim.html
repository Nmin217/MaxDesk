How it works

  1. Two corner lidars each sweep a full 360°; the desk's own body masks rays aimed inward, and the two opposite mounts together cover every direction.
  2. Hits go into a grid map. Obstacles are grown by the desk's real rectangle, once for each of 8 orientations, so the planner knows what fits through a gap and which way the desk must face to do it.
  3. A* searches over position and orientation to a spot about arm's length from you, re-planned 5× per second. It can turn the desk in place wherever there's room and prefers keeping the long axis along the direction of travel. Blue ghost outlines show where it plans to turn.
  4. The follower drives to each planned turn point, stops, rotates to the planned orientation, then carries on, and it won't drive while facing the wrong way for the gap ahead.
  5. Person classification: each tracked blob has a still timer. Moving → wait. Still <5 s → wait; they might move again. Still >5 s, or never moved → routable; the path bends around them.
  6. Speed is capped by a stopping-distance model (v = √(2·a·gap)) over the swept corridor.
  7. If the desk commands motion but doesn't move for a second, recovery kicks in: rotate to align, back off, or spin to re-scan.
  8. The desk no longer lowers before driving. It stays at your height for the whole trip, so nothing has to be wired into the Humanscale controls. A tall desk is top-heavy, so keep loose items off it and expect the speed and braking limits to matter more on real hardware.
  9. Acceleration is capped well below the slip threshold of typical desk items.
  10. Safety supervisor: just before the wheels, the desk's real 2-by-1 footprint is swept along its motion against the live lidar points, and speed is capped so it can always stop in time. Yaw that would swing a corner into something is refused. If no route fits with the full margin, it re-plans with a tighter one.
  11. Parking: the desk's forward is the back of its monitor, so you stand on the keyboard side. It aims for the spot directly above or below you, so the table ends up horizontal on screen, then turns to face you (horizontal when it can, broadside to you otherwise). It turns while it drives (swerve), and only stops for a turn if the plan has no room to do it on the move.
  12. Charging: the start spot is a dock pad. When the desk is parked there, still and facing forward, charging starts by itself for both the robot battery and the UPS battery. If either gets low while it's idle, it drives itself back to the dock.
  13. Getting stuck: a short align / back-off / spin first. If it gets stuck again soon after, it backs out along the trail it came in on (those positions and orientations are known to be clear) and re-plans. If a moving person has held it up for ~8 s without clearing, it stops waiting and routes around them.
  14. Map memory: obstacles the lidar sees steadily are remembered between trips (and between visits, in this browser) until a later lidar ray passes through that spot. The planner prefers routes through space it has already seen and avoids places where people often are. Learn the room drives a mapping tour; Forget map wipes it. Tick Costmap to see what it knows: purple = remembered obstacles, orange = busy, grey = never seen.
  15. Charging station (manual cord): pick Manual cord and use Plug in cord / Unplug cord. With the interlock on, the wheels get no command at all while the cord is plugged in (any trip is cancelled, even one already under way) and the desk flashes an alert. Turn the interlock off to see the second layer: the breakaway connector lets go at about 0.9 m and the trip continues. Auto contacts keeps the earlier hands-free dock.
  16. Return: Return to start sends it back to the dashed outline with the same lowering, driving, safety and turning logic, and it parks there horizontal with the monitor's back forward. The checkbox does it automatically 10 s after it has arrived and raised.

Concept model - drag to rotate

  • Standing desk top on lift columns, over an FRC-style frame of 2x1 aluminum tube. Four 3 in MAXSwerve modules sit inboard of the rails, drawn to REV's published dimensions (133.1 mm tabs, 100.5 mm body, 171.5 mm tall with the NEO, 3 in x 7/8 in wheel). Motor sizes and plate details are approximate. Use Zoom to a swerve module for a close look.
  • Line-interactive UPS bolted under the desktop, so it rides up and down with the desk. It powers the computer, lidars and desk and recharges itself whenever the dock gives it wall power. It adds to the load on the lift columns and raises the center of gravity a little, compared with mounting it low in the frame.
  • Robot's own FRC 12V pack on the front rail, drivetrain only.
  • Two lidars on outriggers at opposite frame corners, at a fixed low height. They do not move when the desk height changes, so they always scan the same plane.
  • Red E-stop on top. Wire it to cut wheels and desk motors in hardware, not code.
